## Answer first

As of their June 2026 meetings, **both the Fed and the Bank of England are on hold at 3.75%, and both are leaning hawkish** — the near-term risk is skewed toward a further hike, not a cut, driven by above-target inflation tied to an energy-price shock and Middle East uncertainty. The Fed's hawkish tilt is the more pronounced: on 17 June 2026 it held the funds target at 3.50%–3.75% but raised its dot-plot median for end-2026 to 3.8% (from 3.4% in March), with nine of eighteen officials now projecting at least one hike, and markets subsequently pricing a possible hike as early as October (WRS-001, WRS-002, WRS-003). The BoE also held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 17 June 2026, but by a 7–2 vote (two members wanted a 25bp hike to 4.00%), and the market's central case there is still an extended hold through the rest of 2026 rather than a hike (WRS-006, WRS-009). The key contrast: the Fed is closer to actually hiking, while the BoE's base case remains a prolonged hold with a hawkish minority.

## What the sources say

### Sub-question 1 — Most recent decision, direction, and rationale

**US Federal Reserve (FOMC, 16–17 June 2026):**
- Held the federal funds target range at 3.50%–3.75% (upper bound 3.75%); the decision was unanimous — WRS-001, WRS-003. This matches the level registered as SRC-002.
- Rationale: the statement said "economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East," that "job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little," and that "inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy" — WRS-001, WRS-003.
- Tone shift: the June statement dropped prior language signalling a bias toward future cuts and was markedly shorter (~130 words vs. 341 in the 29 April release), read by reporters as a hawkish reframing — WRS-003.

**Bank of England (MPC, meeting ending 17 June 2026):**
- Held Bank Rate at 3.75%, the fourth consecutive hold, by a majority of 7–2 — WRS-006, WRS-007, WRS-008. This matches the level registered as SRC-003.
- The two dissenters, Megan Greene and Huw Pill, voted to raise Bank Rate by 25bp to 4.00% — WRS-006, WRS-008.
- Rationale: CPI inflation was 2.8% in May 2026, above the 2% target; global energy prices had fallen since the previous meeting in response to Middle East events but remained higher than pre-conflict and volatile; the majority judged a hold appropriate while watching whether energy-driven "second-round effects" embed in wages and prices — WRS-006, WRS-007.

### Sub-question 2 — Near-term signal (guidance, dot plot / vote split, market expectations)

**Federal Reserve:**
- The June Summary of Economic Projections raised the median end-2026 funds-rate dot to 3.8%, up from 3.4% in March — implying at least one hike this year — WRS-002, WRS-003.
- Distribution among the 18 participants: nine projected at least one hike, eight no change, and one a cut — WRS-002, WRS-003, WRS-005. (Reported more granularly as: one for +75bp cumulative, five for +50bp, three for +25bp, eight unchanged, one for −25bp — WRS-002.)
- Inflation projections were raised: headline PCE seen at 3.6% at end-2026 (from 2.7% in March) and core at 3.3% — WRS-002, WRS-003.
- Market pricing: ahead of the decision markets expected no 2026 cuts and roughly one hike by year-end; after the decision and the post-meeting press conference (attributed to Chair Warsh in the reporting), traders moved to price a hike potentially as early as October — WRS-003, WRS-004.

**Bank of England:**
- The June Market Participants Survey (MaPS) median had expected Bank Rate to stay unchanged at this meeting and to remain unchanged over the following year — WRS-006.
- The next scheduled decision is 30 July 2026 — WRS-006, WRS-009.
- As of ~14 July 2026, financial markets still expected the BoE to hold at 3.75% for the rest of 2026, though the perceived likelihood of a rise had increased following renewed Middle East hostilities — WRS-009.
- Among the holders, Catherine Mann voted to hold but signalled she is actively evaluating whether to act, noting a forceful decision can move inflation quickly and that she is watching business pricing and 2027 wage settlements — WRS-006, WRS-008.

### Sub-question 3 — Notable contrast between Fed and BoE

- Both banks are at the **same headline level (3.75%)** and both **held in June 2026 with a hawkish tilt** rooted in energy-linked inflation and Middle East uncertainty — WRS-001, WRS-003, WRS-006, WRS-007.
- The **Fed is the more hawkish and closer to hiking**: its dot plot flipped to signal a hike (median 3.8%), half its members project a hike, it dropped its easing bias, and markets moved to price a possible October hike — WRS-002, WRS-003, WRS-005.
- The **BoE is more balanced / on an extended hold**: the majority held 7–2 with only two members seeking a hike, and the market's central case remains a hold through the rest of 2026, reflecting the MPC balancing above-target inflation against weaker UK growth — WRS-006, WRS-009.
- Net: for the board's purposes, both peers sit at 3.75% and neither is expected to cut in the near term; the direction-of-travel risk at both is toward a hike, more firmly so at the Fed — WRS-002, WRS-003, WRS-006, WRS-009.
